What this review establishes

These six designs explore copy, composition and a proposed presentation of product results. They use designed interface examples, not native app captures. The owner authorized these concepts while production work continues. App readiness and conversion lift are not established by this gallery.

A and B carry the same benefits and sample content. A leads with a recognizable human situation; B gives the proposed interface more space. All images and ZIPs are labelled concepts and must be replaced or verified before App Store use.

A01 / B01

Reflux questions. Clearer answers.

Make sense of your next question.

The opening frame connects a recurring situation to a specific answer. It makes the assistant’s value visible before asking people to care about tracking. The lead is a hypothesis based on the Brian direction, not measured conversion data.

A02 / B02

Understand your test results.

Medical terms. Everyday language.

A concrete result gives the explanation benefit a visible before-and-after: unfamiliar numbers become a readable explanation and a useful question. The B12 value and reference interval are synthetic demonstration data, not a real patient record or a universal normal range. A value of 220 pg/mL is not labelled reassuring or definitively normal.

A03 / B03

Food choices. Less guesswork.

Visual system

Cream #F5F1EA, deep teal #18524F, mint #5BA89B and restrained gold #BC955C. Existing V5 lab and restaurant photographs retain visual continuity. Frame 01 reuses the earlier V3 phone-reading scene as a visual candidate. Generated people are illustrative, not customers or testimonials.

Typography, phone frames and interface elements are composed in HTML/CSS. No readable interface is generated inside a photograph. The original PNGs are 1320 × 2868 pixels, RGB, without transparency. The review footer on each image identifies the concept status.
